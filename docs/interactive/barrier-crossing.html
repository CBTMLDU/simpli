1.38 kcal/mol

is what it costs to make the wait ten times longer, at kBT = 0.6. That is just kBT · ln 10 — the straight line in the plot, measured from your own runs.

Start at a low barrier and watch. The particle rattles inside one well and hops across every so often. Now push the barrier slider to the right, one notch at a time, and watch how quickly the hopping stops.
What is being simulated. One particle on a double well, V(x) = h(x² − 1)², moving by overdamped Langevin dynamics: a downhill push plus a random thermal kick each step, with kBT = 0.6 kcal/mol. Steps are the clock here, scaled so that every barrier height gets the same number of attempts per step. The absolute time per step is arbitrary; the way the waiting time scales with h is not. That scaling is the whole problem.
