Error over time

Plain deposition bottoms out, then drifts back up. Well-tempered keeps decaying.

Start in Plain and let it fill. Bias piles up wherever the particle sits, the well it started in slowly floods, and it spills into the next one. Watch the error trace on the right once all three wells are full — then switch to Well-tempered and start over.
What is being deposited. A Gaussian of height w and width σ dropped at the particle's position every 200 steps, added to a running bias B(x) that the particle also feels. In plain metadynamics every hill is the same size. In well-tempered, the height is scaled by exp(−B(x)/kBΔT), so deposition slows as a region fills and the estimate −B·γ/(γ−1) converges instead of overshooting. Left running long enough, plain deposition reaches its best answer and then walks away from it.
